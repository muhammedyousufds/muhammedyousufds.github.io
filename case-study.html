Public-source reconciliation question · not an error finding

Pro-Forma Cash and Note 16 balances

GFG’s Pro-Forma Cash bridge is €185.1m: €176.5m of cash and cash equivalents plus €8.6m of restricted cash and receivables from deposits; investment funds are nil. Note 16 separately lists €6.2m of current restricted cash and €3.3m of current receivables from deposits, a combined €9.5m.

The €0.9m difference is not directly reconciled line by line in the published notes. The KPI and Note 16 amounts may have different scope or eligibility; Note 16 says some balances relate to collateral and deposits. Public figures alone cannot determine the reason, and this is not evidence of misstatement.

Sources: Annual report · Note 16, p. 84 · Pro-Forma Cash definition, p. 129

Illustrative reasoning · three examples shown

From disclosure to a question worth testing

Each example separates what the report says from what might merit follow-up. The procedures below are proposals only; they were not performed.

Inventory valuation

Could inventory be carried above the amount expected to be recovered through sale?

Example A · IAS 2

Public signal

Net inventory fell from €96.4m to €81.9m. The provision fell from €9.3m on €105.2m gross inventory to €7.1m on €88.3m. Provision coverage decreased from 8.84% to 8.04% (−0.80 percentage points).

Question and risk

A lower rate may be reasonable if stock mix, ageing or expected selling prices improved; it may merit follow-up if markdown exposure worsened. The potential risk is inventory overstated above net realisable value. Assertion: valuation.

Evidence a real audit would need

Ageing, subsequent sales, realised selling prices versus cost, markdowns and the NRV model. Public disclosures do not supply item-level records; no such procedures were performed here.

Source: GFG Annual Report 2025 · Note 15, p. 83.

Revenue, marketplace and returns

When does GFG control the promised good or service, and should revenue be shown gross or net?

Example B · IFRS 15

Public signal

The report separates €562.4m of goods revenue and €88.6m of Marketplace revenue. Marketplace is 39.3% of NMV but 13.0% of IFRS revenue; those measures have different bases. The report also discloses refund liabilities and a return-recovery asset.

Question and risk

Contracts determine whether GFG acts as principal or agent; delivery, returns and customer obligations affect timing and measurement. Potential risks include gross-versus-net presentation, cut-off and return estimates. Assertions include occurrence, accuracy, cut-off and completeness of refund liabilities.

Evidence a real audit would need

Seller/platform contracts, order and delivery records, settlement data, subsequent returns and refund-estimate support. Public summaries cannot establish contract terms or order-level cut-off; no procedures were performed.

Sources: Notes 14–15, pp. 83–84 · Note 24, p. 93.

Impairment and forecast sensitivity

How much headroom remains if important assumptions change?

Example C · IAS 36

Public signal

The published ANZ recoverable amount is €113.4m and headroom is €43.2m. Break-even movements include an 8.0 percentage-point higher discount rate, a 3.0 percentage-point lower EBITDA margin, 26.9% higher capital expenditure, or 3.3% lower revenue growth.

Question and risk

Recoverability depends on forecast cash flows and assumptions such as growth, margins, investment and discount rates. The potential risk is that goodwill or other assets are overstated if IAS 36 assumptions are unsupported. Assertions: valuation, allocation and disclosure.

Evidence a real audit would need

Approved forecasts, historical forecast accuracy, external evidence, model arithmetic and support for discount-rate inputs. Public data is insufficient to recreate cash flows or validate assumptions; no valuation or impairment test was performed.

Source: GFG Annual Report 2025 · Note 13, pp. 80–83.
