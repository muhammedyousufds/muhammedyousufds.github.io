Analysis chain

Keep each step visible.

A disclosure movement is not automatically a risk, and a risk hypothesis is not a finding of misstatement.

  1. Published sourceIdentify the report, note, page, period, unit and reporting perimeter.
  2. Reported figureRecord the company-published amount without changing its basis.
  3. CalculationShow the formula and distinguish it from company-reported metrics.
  4. ObservationDescribe the direction or relationship in neutral terms.
  5. HypothesisState a plausible explanation that needs evidence to evaluate.
  6. Potential riskConnect the hypothesis to a possible financial-statement exposure.
  7. AssertionIdentify what could be misstated: e.g., valuation, completeness or cut-off.
  8. Proposed responseDescribe work that could address the risk in a real engagement; label it proposed.
  9. Evidence limitState what public information cannot establish and what records would be needed.

Priority labels

P1 and P2 are author-assigned investigation priorities in the independent assessment. The public materials do not document a sufficiently detailed scoring rubric to treat these as calibrated audit risk ratings. They are not audit materiality conclusions and do not imply that a misstatement exists.

AI review

AI assists selected steps; people own the judgement.

AI assisted with organising public disclosures and drafting selected analytical observations. Python and Excel performed defined calculations and cross-checks. I checked material statements against the annual report and re-performed calculations. Unsupported suggestions were narrowed or left unresolved; the €0.9m Note 16/APM cash difference remains unallocated. Human judgement was required to distinguish reported facts, calculations, observations, hypotheses, risks and possible responses. AI cannot establish transaction-level facts, audit evidence, a control conclusion or whether an estimate is adequate.

KAM firewall

Freeze the baseline before looking across.

  1. PreserveRetain the original independent assessment unchanged; make any explanatory copy separately.
  2. IdentifyPublish its file name, version and checksum so readers can identify the baseline bytes.
  3. Qualify timingA checksum identifies a version but does not establish the historical date it was frozen. Use timestamp evidence only if it exists and can be inspected.
  4. Compare separatelyKeep the published-auditor comparison in a separate file and label matches as thematic classifications.
  5. Learn without rewritingDiscuss differences and lessons in a new reflection. Do not edit baseline risks to increase apparent alignment.

Interpretation limit: KAMs are auditor-selected matters reported for the audited financial statements. Similar wording does not mean endorsement; non-matching themes do not show that the independent assessment was wrong; and the comparison is not an evaluation of audit quality.

Accounting labels

Reported, calculated and management-defined are different.

Reported IFRS amount

A figure presented in the company’s IFRS financial statements or notes, with its source and period cited.

Calculated metric

A ratio or movement calculated from disclosed amounts. Publish the formula, inputs, rounding and any perimeter caveat.

Alternative performance measure

A management-defined measure such as Pro-Forma Cash or net working capital. Preserve the company’s definition and reconciliation; do not relabel it as an IFRS line item.
