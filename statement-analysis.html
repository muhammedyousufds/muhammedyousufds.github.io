How to read this

Revenue fell in euros; the constant-currency movement was smaller.

Reported: €679.8m versus €722.6m, a calculated 5.9% decline in reported euros.

Question: What explains the difference between the reported-euro and constant-currency declines, and what do revenue trends by region show? Public totals alone can’t confirm the cause.

What stands out

Translation gains narrowed the total comprehensive loss.

FY2025: GFG reported a €62.2m loss. A €3.1m foreign-currency translation gain in other comprehensive income reduced the total comprehensive loss to €59.1m.

Interpretation: This gain arises when the Group’s overseas operations are translated into euros. It is recorded in other comprehensive income and the foreign-currency translation reserve—not as revenue or operating profit.

Question: Does the €3.1m gain reconcile to the movement in the foreign-currency translation reserve and the exchange rates used for the Group’s overseas operations?

What stands out

Inventory fell, while the short-term liquidity buffer narrowed.

Inventory: fell by €14.5m, or 15.0%. GFG links its improved gross margin to a healthier inventory profile, but the totals alone cannot validate valuation or provisions.

Liquidity: the current ratio fell from 1.32x to 1.06x. Trade and other receivables fell €27.7m, mainly due to increased factoring. Year-end cash included €6.2m of restricted cash, while €40.4m of convertible bonds were classified as current because holders could redeem them on 15 March 2026.

Further evidence: inventory ageing and post-year-end sales; factoring terms; cash availability; and the bond redemption terms.

How cash moved

Operating cash flow improved, while reported cash declined.

Operating activities: net cash improved from a €15.0m outflow to a €7.5m inflow. The cash-flow statement shows a positive €26.7m adjustment from lower trade receivables. GFG separately reports that trade and other receivables fell €27.7m, mainly due to increased factoring, so the movement should not be read as improved customer collections alone.

Investing activities: FY2024 included €161.9m from redemption of investment funds. FY2025 had no equivalent inflow and included €14.0m of purchases of PPE and intangible assets.

Financing: bond repurchases were €13.3m in FY2025 versus €104.3m in FY2024. These are cash-flow movements, not recurring operating costs.

Perimeter: the cash-flow statement includes continuing and discontinued operations, unlike the continuing-operations headline metrics.

How to read this

The FY2025 loss drove most of the €55.6m decline in total equity.

Movement: total equity fell from €211.2m to €155.6m. The €62.2m loss was partly offset by €3.1m of other comprehensive income, a €1.9m increase in the share-based payment reserve and a €1.6m change in non-controlling interests.

Analysis focus: connect the statement to the loss and OCI statements, then trace material changes in ownership, reserves and convertible-bond components to the relevant notes.

Evidence boundary: this bridge explains the reported movement; it does not test the underlying transactions or valuations.

No standalone ratio is used for changes in equity: this statement reconciles movements between opening and closing balances. Component balances appear in the financial-position statement; the linked report contains the full reserve-by-reserve rollforward.
