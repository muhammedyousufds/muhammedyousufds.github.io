Reading the numbers correctly

FY2025 revenue was €679.8m against €722.6m in FY2024: a calculated 5.92% decline in reported euros. GFG separately reported a 0.6% decline on a constant-currency basis. The two figures answer different questions; the constant-currency measure adjusts for exchange-rate movements and does not replace reported euro revenue.

Calculated gross margin was 46.41% in FY2025 and 44.89% in FY2024. Reported net inventory was €81.9m and €96.4m respectively. These movements are observations, not evidence of error. They may inform questions about product mix, markdowns, returns, purchasing and the net realisable value of inventory.

Period and perimeter matter: the FY2025 report presents profit-or-loss comparatives on a continuing-operations basis; the consolidated cash-flow statement includes continuing and discontinued operations. GFG’s financial KPIs exclude Argentina and Chile. These series should not be treated as interchangeable.

Illustrative reasoning

From disclosure to a question worth testing.

These examples describe analysis performed on public disclosures and possible work that could follow. Proposed responses are not completed procedures.

Example A · IAS 2

Inventory valuation

Could inventory be carried above the amount expected to be recovered through sale?

  1. Source factReported net inventory decreased from €96.4m in FY2024 to €81.9m in FY2025. The disclosed inventory provision decreased from €9.3m on €105.2m gross inventory to €7.1m on €88.3m.
  2. CalculationProvision-to-gross-inventory ratio: 8.84% in FY2024 and 8.04% in FY2025 (calculated from disclosed amounts).
  3. ObservationBoth gross inventory and the provision fell; the provision ratio also fell by about 0.80 percentage points.
  4. HypothesisA lower provision rate could be reasonable if stock mix, ageing or expected selling prices improved; it could merit follow-up if markdown exposure or ageing worsened.
  5. Potential riskUnder IAS 2, inventory may be overstated if cost exceeds net realisable value (NRV), after considering estimated selling price less completion and selling costs.
  6. AssertionValuation and allocation of inventory.
  7. Proposed responseFor a real engagement, inspect ageing and subsequent sales, compare realised selling prices with cost, test markdowns and challenge the basis and completeness of the NRV provision.
  8. Evidence limitationPublic disclosures do not provide item-level ageing, cost layers, post-year-end sales or the company’s detailed NRV model. No such procedures were performed here.
Example B · IFRS 15

Revenue, marketplace and returns

When does the group control the promised good or service, and should revenue be shown gross or net?

  1. Source factThe FY2025 disclosure distinguishes €562.4m of goods revenue and €88.6m of Marketplace revenue. Marketplace represented 39.3% of NMV and 13.0% of IFRS revenue. Disclosures also include refund liabilities, return recovery assets and contract liabilities.
  2. CalculationMarketplace IFRS revenue is a smaller share than its NMV share; these disclosed measures have different bases and should not be mistaken for a direct margin comparison.
  3. ObservationRetail and marketplace transactions involve different fulfilment and contractual roles; customer returns and gift-card balances also affect timing and measurement.
  4. HypothesisIf the platform controls a specified good or service before transfer, it may be principal; if it arranges for another party to provide it, it may be agent. Returns estimates or cut-off judgements could also be sensitive.
  5. Potential riskRevenue could be misstated in gross-versus-net presentation, timing, returns or customer obligations if accounting conclusions do not reflect the contract and transfer of control.
  6. AssertionOccurrence, accuracy, cut-off, classification and presentation; completeness and valuation of refund liabilities and related assets.
  7. Proposed responseFor a real engagement, inspect seller/platform contracts, map obligations, trace orders to dispatch/delivery and settlement, test year-end transactions, inspect subsequent returns and recalculate refund estimates.
  8. Evidence limitationPublished summaries cannot establish the contractual rights, system timestamps, order-level delivery, settlement or return patterns needed to conclude on principal-agent status or cut-off.
Example C · IAS 36

Impairment and forecast sensitivity

How much headroom remains if important assumptions change?

  1. Source factThe published disclosure reports ANZ recoverable amount of €113.4m and headroom of €43.2m, with one-variable break-even sensitivities including an 8.0 percentage-point higher discount rate, a 3.0 percentage-point lower EBITDA margin, and a 26.9% capex increase.
  2. CalculationThe disclosure supplies its own headroom and threshold sensitivities. No new company valuation is calculated in this portfolio example.
  3. ObservationRecoverability depends on forecast cash flows and assumptions such as revenue growth, margins, investment and discount rates.
  4. HypothesisIf plausible downside assumptions materially reduce headroom, the carrying amount could be sensitive to estimation uncertainty.
  5. Potential riskGoodwill or other assets may be overstated if value-in-use or fair-value assumptions are not supportable under IAS 36.
  6. AssertionValuation and allocation; presentation and disclosure.
  7. Proposed responseFor a real engagement, challenge approved forecasts against historical performance and external evidence, test model arithmetic, assess discount-rate inputs, and independently recalculate sensitivities.
  8. Evidence limitationPublished information does not provide enough detail to recreate all forecast cash flows or independently validate assumptions. Any scenario added later must be clearly marked synthetic and must not be presented as GFG’s forecast or calculation.

Sources: GFG Annual Report 2025 and FY2025 results announcement. Values above reflect the selected case-study’s source schedule; a reviewer should consult the cited annual report notes and definitions.

KAM firewall

Separate analysis, then comparison.

The independent risk assessment was frozen before the separate comparison with EY’s published FY2025 Key Audit Matters. The comparison grouped themes as three clear, two partial and four no match; a tax KAM was additional to the independent categories. These classifications describe thematic overlap only. They do not show that EY endorsed this project, and they do not assess audit quality.
